**Humbug**  
   - **Definition**: Deceptive or pretentious language or behavior; nonsense or a trick.  
   - **Common Usage**: Fairly uncommon in daily life but often encountered in literature or discussions about deceit.  
   - **Examples**:  
     - The salesman’s promise of a miraculous product was nothing but humbug.  
     - “Bah, humbug!” declared Scrooge, dismissing the holiday spirit.  
   - **Alternatives**: Nonsense, sham, fraud.  

---  

**Marquee**  
   - **Definition**: A large tent used for outdoor events; also refers to a sign over the entrance of a theater or building, often containing the title of a show or event.  
   - **Common Usage**: Commonly used in event planning or when discussing theaters; permanent canopy over an entrance of a hotel etc; also found in marketing contexts.  
   - **Examples**:  
     - The wedding took place under a beautifully decorated marquee in the garden.  
     - The marquee at the theater displayed the names of the latest blockbuster movies.  
   - **Alternatives**: Tent (often sumptuous tent), signboard, display.  

---  

**Smarmy**  
   - **Definition**: Excessively flattering or ingratiating; insincere charm.  
   - **Common Usage**: Somewhat common in conversations when discussing behavior or personality, often with a negative connotation.  
   - **Examples**:  
     - His smarmy compliments made everyone feel uneasy about his intentions.  
     - The politician had a smarmy demeanor that turned off many voters.  
   - **Alternatives**: Sycophantic, insincere, ingratiating.  

---  

**Morose**  
   - **Definition**: Gloomy or sullen, often in a manner that is withdrawn or unhappy.  
   - **Common Usage**: Fairly common in discussions about mood, personality, or emotional state.  
   - **Examples**:  
     - After hearing the bad news, he became morose and didn’t want to talk to anyone.  
     - The morose atmosphere of the room was palpable.  
   - **Alternatives**: Sullen, gloomy, despondent.  

---  

**Prosaic**  
   - **Definition**: Lacking poetic beauty; commonplace or unromantic; dull.  
   - **Common Usage**: Somewhat common in literary contexts when discussing writing styles or descriptions.  
   - **Examples**:  
     - The prosaic details of the report made it difficult to stay engaged.  
     - His prosaic approach to storytelling failed to capture the audience’s imagination.  
   - **Alternatives**: Mundane, ordinary, pedestrian.  

---  

**scupper**  
   - **Definition**: To obstruct or prevent something from happening; to sabotage.  
   - **Common Usage**: Less common, primarily used in more specific or casual contexts, such as conversations about plans or projects.  
   - **Examples**:  
     - The unexpected rain was enough to scupper our plans for a picnic.  
     - A power outage scuppered the final performance of the play.  
   - **Alternatives**: Ruin, thwart, thwart.  

---  

**Hackneyed**  
   - **Definition**: Lacking in originality or freshness; overused and trite.  
   - **Common Usage**: Common, especially in literary and artistic contexts when critiquing clichés or overused ideas.  
   - **Examples**:  
     - The movie was filled with hackneyed plot twists that made it predictable.  
     - The phrase "time heals all wounds" has become so hackneyed that it's lost its meaning.  
   - **Alternatives**: Trite, cliché, stale.  

---  

**Maraud**  
   - **Definition**: To roam or raid in search of plunder.  
   - **Common Usage**: Less common, often used in historical contexts or literature to describe acts of pillaging or looting.  
   - **Examples**:  
     - The pirates would maraud along the coast, taking whatever they wanted.  
     - During the war, soldiers were known to maraud the nearby villages for supplies.  
   - **Alternatives**: Pillage, plunder, raid.  

---  

**Tizzy**  
   - **Definition**: A state of nervous excitement or agitation.  
   - **Common Usage**: Common in informal conversations when describing someone who is overreacting or becoming overly excited.  
   - **Examples**:  
     - She was in a tizzy trying to find her lost keys before the meeting.  
     - The children were in a tizzy about the upcoming holiday.  
   - **Alternatives**: Frenzy, fluster, commotion.  

---  

**Macerate**  
   - **Definition**: To soften or break down a substance by soaking it in a liquid.  
   - **Common Usage**: Common in culinary contexts, particularly in cooking or food preparation.  
   - **Examples**:  
     - You should macerate the strawberries in sugar to **bring out** their sweetness.  
     - The recipe called for macerating the herbs in olive oil for added flavor.  
   - **Alternatives**: Soak, soften, steep.  

---  

**Vamoose**  
   - **Definition**: To depart quickly; to leave hastily.  
   - **Common Usage**: Informal and somewhat colloquial; used in casual conversations primarily.  
   - **Examples**:  
     - It was getting late, so we decided it was time to vamoose.  
     - The cat vamoosed as soon as it heard the doorbell.  
   - **Alternatives**: Depart, leave, scram.
